Giardino pubblico Muzio De Tommasini

The public garden Muzio de' Tommasini (locally abbreviated as public garden or giardino pubblico) is a park in the city of Trieste, named after the same political and botanical figure.

Nice to know:
  • Entrances: Via Giulia, Via Marconi, Via Volta
  • Opening hours: 07:00-20:00 (estivo); 07:00-19:00 (invernale)
  • Surface: 0,03 km^2

History

Opened in 1864 and named after the podestà of Trieste Muzio de Tommasini, it hosts 368 native and exotic trees, a lake and a children's playground. Along the avenues are 31 busts of illustrious citizens: actors, politicians, artists, archaeologists and, of course, in large numbers, poets and writers.

Among the busts, in addition to Svevo, Joyce, Saba, Slataper, Stuparich, Kosovel, Giotti and Kugy, we remember Valentino and Anita Pittoni (the uncle, socialist politician, and the granddaughter-writer), the journalist Silvio Benco, the painter Umberto Veruda, the historian Pietro Kandler and the Albanian-born actor Alessandro Moissi.

According to a local tradition, in the autumn of 1912 the park was the setting for a curious episode that remained in the memory of its visitors. It is said that, during an evening stroll, a group of Trieste students organized a small literary circle outdoors, choosing a bench overlooking the lake as their meeting place. Each participant was invited to leave a short piece of writing on a sheet of paper, which was then hidden inside the pages of an old book. For years, the book was supposedly passed from one regular visitor to another, gradually collecting poems, stories and anonymous thoughts. Although there are no documents confirming the episode, the legend is still remembered as one of the many curious stories associated with the history of the park.
